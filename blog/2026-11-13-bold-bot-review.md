## The short answer

Bold Bot is a real product, sold by a real company, Bold Analysis, at a price it states openly. In that sense it's legit.

## Five questions to ask any trading bot

These apply to Bold Bot, to every other bot vendor, and to us.

### 1. Is there a live record someone else can check?

A backtest shows what a set of rules would have done on past data. A screenshot shows whatever the person taking it chose to show. Neither tells you what happened in a real account over time. Stronger evidence: broker statements, a record kept by a third party, or a live record on a platform the vendor can't edit, with the losing trades left in.

### 2. How big is the stop compared to the target?

This part is arithmetic, not opinion. A strategy that risks 250 ticks to make 35 has to win about 88% of its trades just to break even, before commissions and slippage:

> Break-even win rate = stop ÷ (stop + target) = 250 ÷ (250 + 35) ≈ 87.7%

Wide-stop, small-target systems can look excellent for weeks, because most trades win. The risk sits in the losers: each full loss can erase about seven winners. Ask for the largest losing trade and the worst drawdown, not just the win rate.

We're using the numbers one NexusFi reviewer reported to show the math. We don't know Bold Bot's current settings, so ask the vendor.

### 3. What happens when it stops working?

Markets change. Volatility, trends and the way people trade all shift, and a strategy tuned to one stretch of the market can fade in the next. That happens to strategies run by professionals too. Before you subscribe, ask how the vendor knows when a strategy has stopped working, what they change when it does, and whether you'll get to see the record of the bad stretches.

### 4. How much does the fee need to earn?

At $1,000 a month, a bot costs $12,000 a year before commissions, data and platform fees. On a $25,000 account, the strategy has to make 48% of the account each year just to cover its own cost. That's not a verdict on the bot. It's a number to put next to your account size.

### 5. Can you run it on paper first?

A few weeks on a simulated account won't prove a strategy works. It will show you how it trades: how often, how big the swings are, and whether you can live with them.
